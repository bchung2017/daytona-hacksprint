Math breakdown

Every credit has its own math. None of it runs on a laptop.

1/

There is no one formula. Hire a Veteran is 15% of wages with a cap that halves for part-time. WOTC is 40% of the first $14k. The Investment Tax Credit is 5% of equipment cost. Each one is its own little program.

2/

So TaxBeagle writes a small program per credit, per person, from the quoted rules. It never runs it here. Daytona spins up a sealed sandbox: no network, no keys, nothing but the inputs. Run, read the number, throw the box away.

3/

The rules it quotes come from the real government pages, fetched through Anchor's cloud browser. A fact only counts if the quote is found word for word in the page. No paraphrase, no memory.

4/

The result, the quotes and the CPA's number all land in SurrealDB. The database itself holds the gate: a claim can only become "ready" if the CPA's figure matches ours. Python can't override it.

5/

Live-query from SurrealDB pushes every state change to this screen. What you're watching is the record, not a progress bar.

6/

Net: one credit, one sandbox, one quoted rule set, one independent CPA, one database gate, one human click. That's the whole trick.

Other adjustments a CPA might raise

  • Part-time vs full-time. "She was under 1,820 hours once unpaid leave comes out, so the part-time cap applies." The credit halves.
  • Non-qualified pay. "That $84,000 includes a $6,000 signing bonus. Qualified wages are $78,000." The figure drops to $11,700.
  • Disabled-veteran rate. "He has a VA disability rating on file. You're using the standard rate; it should be the higher one." The credit goes up, not down.
  • Documentation gap. "No DD-214 in the file yet. I can't sign off until I see it." Held as needs-info, nothing changes until the paperwork lands.
  • Prior employment. "He did contract work for your sister company last year. That may break the new-hire rule." Rejected, with a request for a fresh look at the related-entity test.
  • Hire window. "Her start date is before the program's window for this tax year." Not eligible this year, possibly eligible next.
  • Wrong tax year. "You're claiming on 2025, but first-year wages straddle into 2026. Which year are you filing?" Needs-info until the year is pinned.
